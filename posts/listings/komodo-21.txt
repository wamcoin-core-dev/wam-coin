One value in the entry is deliberately unusual, and I would rather name it
than have it found later. `required_confirmations` is 60, where most coins in
this file are 3.

The cost of reversing a confirmation is set by hashrate, not by block timing.
WAM's network measured 225 kH/s over the last 120 blocks today, and it falls
by about half in the quiet hours of the night -- that is rentable, and six
confirmations on a chain this size is twelve minutes of exposure. The depth
is the only lever that makes a deposit expensive to reverse, and it works
linearly. It is reviewed publicly against measured hashrate, the last review
and its basis are recorded in the repository, and it comes down when the
hashrate no longer justifies it.

The same reasoning runs the other way for small payments, and we publish that
too: 3 confirmations between two people for a small amount, 20 for an
ordinary one, 60 where value leaves the chain and stops being reversible, 100
above a few thousand WAM. Nobody rents half a network to reverse a payment
worth ten coins, so asking a person to wait for protection they already have
costs them the use of the chain and buys them nothing.
