POST THIS IN #announcements, AS FOUR MESSAGES IN ORDER
======================================================

The bot posts the release itself the moment the tag is published — the first
two paragraphs of the notes, automatically. These four are the other thing:
what solo mining is for, and the fee change. Post them after the bot's
message, not before it.

--------------------------------------------------------------- 1 of 4

**v0.1.10 is out, and the headline is one command.**

```
wam-miner --solo -u YOUR_ADDRESS -t 4
```

You can now mine WAM against your own node with no pool at all. No account,
no operator, nothing between you and the chain. It needs a running wamd with
`server=1` in `wam.conf` and nothing else.

When you find a block you keep the whole 47.50 WAM instead of a share of it,
and you wait longer between them. That is the trade, and it is yours to make.

--------------------------------------------------------------- 2 of 4

**Before you spend any electricity, ask whether it would work:**

```
wam-miner --check -u YOUR_ADDRESS
```

That builds a real block from your node's current template and asks your node
whether it is valid. It hashes nothing, sends nothing, and answers in about a
second. If the node accepts it, everything except the proof of work is right:
the coinbase, the height encoding, the treasury output, the witness
commitment, the merkle root and the transactions.

Two bugs in this code were caught that way before a single hash was computed.
It is the command to run first, and the one to paste here if something looks
wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------- 3 of 4

**Linux, Windows and macOS — and from this release, proved on each.**

Every build now starts a regtest node and runs `--check` on it: the miner
builds a real block from that node's template and the node must accept it.
Hashing and submission are proved separately on hardware we own -- running a
miner on somebody else's build service is against their terms, which this
project learned the expensive way.

Before v0.1.10 both options had been proved on one Linux laptop and shipped
to the other two untested. That is not the same thing as working, and saying
so is cheaper than finding out from you.

Full instructions for all three: https://wamcoin.org/mine

--------------------------------------------------------------- 4 of 4

**And a change on our side: our pool's fee goes from 0% to 1%.**

We set it to 0% when pool.wamcoin.org was the only pool there was. There are
more now, and a pool charging nothing is not really competing with the others
— nobody can offer less than nothing. Matching their rate means you pick a
pool for how it runs rather than for a number that cannot be beaten.

```
pool.wamcoin.org:3333             ours, 1% fee
wam.ariabrain.com:3333
stratum.cryptopickaxe.co.uk:6040
```

Ask their operators about their fees and payout rules — those are theirs to
state, not ours to state for them. All three answered when this was written.

The 1% is taken after the chain's 5% treasury output and goes to an address
that receives nothing else, named on pool.wamcoin.org, so what stays in the
pool wallet is miners' money and can be checked against what the pool says it
owes.

If you mine with us, nothing breaks and there is nothing you need to do.
